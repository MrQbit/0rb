# The 0rb Playbook

**Life with a fully evolved orb — a walkthrough, written as a diary.**

The baselines are the right ones: **S.A.R.A.H.** — the house in Eureka that
runs itself, knows its people, and has opinions — and **JARVIS** — the
partner that anticipates, procures, books, builds alongside you, and never
needs the same instruction twice. A house manager doesn't just dim the
lights: it feeds you, moves you, restocks you, remembers the birthday, and
handles the errand you forgot you had. That's the bar.

0rb's version is quieter and yours: it runs on a box in your closet,
everything it knows lives in files you can read, **every dollar it spends
sits in the same receipts ledger as every light it dims**, and it asks
before it matters.

This playbook shows that life: **Day 0** (the orb arrives), **Day 1** (it
learns the house *and gets its hands*), then a settled stretch — a workday
with lunch ordered in and a ride across town, a maker Saturday where the
filament reorders itself, a birthday it saves, a Sunday of errands you
never ran, a romantic dinner in **and** the version where you go out — and
finally the invisible machinery: the continuous loop, the feedback loop,
memory, **the commerce layer**, and how the orb improves itself.

Capabilities marked ⏳ are the near-horizon (the services layer is the
current frontier — mechanisms in the appendix); everything else ships today.

## Day 0 — The orb arrives

**18:40.** The box comes home. Power, ethernet, done — no app to download
first, no account to create. From a phone on the house Wi-Fi: **orb.local**.

The screen shows a slowly breathing green orb and a QR code. That code is
the whole security model of first contact: *the orb belongs to whoever is
standing in front of it.* Scan, and you're the owner.

**18:43.** It speaks: *"Hello. I'm your orb. What would you like to call
me?"* You keep "Orb." You add Ana by invitation link — she taps it, types
her name, gets her sign-in code; that's her entire enrollment. Then:
*"Here's what I can already see on your network. Nothing is connected
until you say so."* Sonos, LG TV, Brother printer, MacBook. Speakers and
printer work **that instant** — AirPlay and IPP need no pairing. Direct
first, hubs only for what direct can't do.

**18:50 — accounts.** Settings → Apps → **Your accounts**. One tap each,
sign in, done — like linking apps on a TV. Tonight: Spotify, Google (one
consent = Gmail + Calendar + Drive). The orb never sees passwords; tokens
stay sealed on your box, keyed to each person.

**18:55 — Matter.** Pair once with Apple Home; Siri now drives the orb's
world from every Apple device.

**19:00 — the hands.** ⏳ The services drawer: **Uber. Lyft. DoorDash.
Uber Eats. Instacart. Amazon. DigiKey. OpenTable.** Each is the same
one-tap link as Spotify. And immediately below them, the part that makes
it livable — **budgets**:

> *Food delivery: ask under $60, never over $120 without both owners.
> Rides: auto once earned. Consumables (filament, parts, staples): auto
> under $30 after three approvals. Gifts: always ask. Anything else:
> always ask. Hard cap $400/week — the orb stops and says so.*

Money enters the same trust gradient as door locks: **read → reversible →
confirm → never-auto**, budgets per category, every charge a receipt.

## Day 1 — The apprenticeship

- *"We're leaving"* → it asks: away mode locks and darkens; approve? The
  third identical approval, it offers to just do it — **earned autonomy**,
  visible in your profile, revocable in one tap.
- Your coffee order, mentioned once, lands in your personal memory file —
  markdown you can open, edit, delete. Tomorrow: *"flat white, always."*
- Ana tells it she goes by "Ani" — so it does, because **she** asked. It
  never adopts a nickname it merely overheard.
- ⏳ Its first purchase: you say *"order dinner, the usual Thai."* It reads
  back the cart — pad see ew, larb, the extra rice Ana always wants — and
  the total, you say yes, and twenty-five minutes later it tells the
  courier the gate code, flips the porch light on, and chimes once when
  the bag is at the door. The receipt reads: *"DoorDash $41.20 — Thai
  Basil. Undo: not reversible; refund path noted."* Honest to the bone.

## A Tuesday — the workday

**06:20.** The orb has been up all night, quietly — the loop ticked once a
minute (presence, safety, devices, schedules) and found nothing worth
waking anyone for.

**06:58 — sunrise.** "Morning." The **deck** assembles *now*, not at 3am:
weather (74° → 97°), the two mails that matter out of nineteen, today's
calendar — design review 10:00, **Ana's dentist 15:30 across town**, piano
tuner Friday — house anomalies (door-sensor battery 12%… ⏳ *replacement
already in the cart, $9, under your consumables budget: "ordering unless
you object"*), and who's home.

**07:15 — breakfast, cooked.** Hash from last night's sweet potatoes — it
knows the cast iron, knows Ana's cilantro veto (her file, not yours),
narrates timing while your hands are full, catches "add smoked paprika to
the list" mid-sizzle.

**11:40 — lunch, NOT cooked.** Deep in the client repo, you're not
stopping. *"Orb, lunch."* It knows the calculus: 25 minutes of cooking you
don't have, versus — ⏳ *"Three options: leftover risotto (4 min), the
poke place (18 min, $16, you rated it 👍 twice), or Ana's soup order
doubled since she's home at noon (Uber Eats, $31 both)."* — *"Do the
soup."* Ordered, tracked, courier at the door timed to her arrival. One
receipt, filed under Food, $31 against this week's $87.

**14:55 — the ride.** ⏳ *"Orb, Ana needs to be at the dentist at 3:30."*
It already knows — the calendar told it — and it's been watching traffic:
*"Leave by 15:05. Uber's 4 minutes out, $14; Lyft's 9 minutes, $11. Book
the Uber?"* Yes. Her phone gets the car's plate; the orb watches the trip
and quietly holds your 15:00 reminder until she's dropped off safely.
Rides earned autonomy weeks ago — next time it just books and tells her.

**15:20.** "House to away, secure it." Locks, lights, alerts — earned, so
no card. One receipt, one Undo.

**18:00 — arrival.** Presence flips the house home; hallway light because
it's after dusk; the note Ana left at noon delivers itself on the kitchen
Sonos, only because you're the one who walked in.

## A Saturday — the maker day

**08:30 — 3D printing.** The X2D queues two robot chassis brackets and a
guitar wall hook. The printer widget shows camera, layer, temps; the loop
babysits it. And this is the day the playbook earns its keep:

> ⏳ *"Heads-up: this job needs 240 g; the spool has ~180 g left (I've
> been subtracting every job's usage since you weighed it in). Your usual
> PETG is $24 at Atomic, $27 Prime same-day on Amazon. You're out of
> tolerance for the deadline — I'd do same-day. Order?"* — one tap. The
> filament arrives before the spool dies mid-print, which is the entire
> point of a house manager.

The consumables engine is generic: filament by gram-tracking, resistors by
"we're out" utterances at the bench, coffee beans by consumption cadence,
detergent by dishwasher cycles. Everything under the budget threshold just
replenishes — receipted — and the shopping list stops being a list you
manage and becomes a list you *audit*.

**10:00 — electronics.** Workbench mode: pinouts on the big screen, parts
list live ("out of 10k resistors" → ⏳ DigiKey cart, batched with the
buck converters from Tuesday so you pay shipping once — it *batches*
orders, like a person would). Calibration constants you read aloud land in
`projects/robot-arm.md`, dated, superseding June's numbers instead of
erasing them.

**11:30 — robots.** "Flash rev 12 to the arm" — compile on the Spark,
flash over the network, report. The elbow stutters; the orb recalls June's
identical symptom (brownout), checks the bench supply through the smart
plug's power meter, right again. When the fix needs a part: ⏳ *"McMaster
has the 6mm shaft coupler, $8.40, arrives Tuesday — order with the DigiKey
batch?"*

**16:00 — guitar.** "Practice mode": lights warm, deck silent, metronome
at 92 bpm because that's where Wednesday's sweep-picking left off. Backing
track on the Sonos; "again, slower" drops it 8. *"Longest streak this
month."* The feedback loop, applied to you, gently.

**17:00 — piano.** Ana's voice takes over and the orb switches with it —
her pieces, her tempo log, her teacher's Thursday note. Nothing bleeds
between profiles.

## A Thursday — the birthday save

**Ten days out**, the deck's quiet card: *"Your mother's birthday is the
26th. Last year: the orchid (she sent three photos of it in April — it
seems to be alive)."*

⏳ *"Ideas?"* It reads **her** file — the memory of every mention: she took
up watercolors in March, complained her brushes shed, loved the Sorolla
exhibition. *"A Da Vinci watercolor brush set ($38, Amazon, 4.8★), the
Sorolla exhibition catalogue ($45, museum store, ships in 3 days), or
flowers-day-of as the fallback ($60). The catalogue needs ordering by
Friday to arrive in time."* — *"Catalogue, and flowers day-of."*

Gifts are **always-ask** tier, so it confirms the card charge, gift-wraps
where the store offers it, watches both deliveries, and on the 26th the
deck says: *"Catalogue delivered Tuesday (she hasn't opened the tracking —
it's a surprise). Flowers arrive 10am. Call her after 11, she has yoga."*
Afterward it files what worked, because next year's suggestion should be
better than this year's.

That's the JARVIS bar: not "reminder: birthday." The save.

## A Sunday — the errands you didn't run

- **Groceries:** the staples list crossed its threshold; ⏳ the Instacart
  cart is built from the list *plus* the gaps it inferred (you cooked the
  last eggs Thursday). You review 30 seconds, remove one impulse item it
  learned you always remove (next week it stops adding it), approve.
  Delivery lands in the 4–6pm window it knows you're home.
- **The return:** the shirt from last week doesn't fit. *"Orb, return
  this."* ⏳ Return initiated, QR label printed on the Brother without
  drivers ever having been installed, pickup scheduled with tomorrow's
  courier, refund watched until it posts — then the receipt closes itself.
- **The refill:** ⏳ pharmacy says the prescription has one refill left;
  the orb queues it and puts the pickup on the deck for your Tuesday drive
  — batched with the dry cleaning two doors down, because it knows both
  addresses and thinks in *trips*, not tasks.
- **The audit:** first Sunday of the month, the deck carries the spend
  card: *"Food $214, rides $61, consumables $84, gifts $45. Two
  subscriptions renewed; one (the streaming service nobody has opened in
  60 days) is worth cancelling — want me to?"* The house manager manages
  the books, too.

## A Friday evening — the romantic dinner, both endings

**You, at lunch:** *"Orb — dinner for two tonight. The good one."*

**Ending A — in.** The menu comes from what's actually in the kitchen plus
one gap ⏳ it had couriered by 5pm (saffron — approved from your phone,
$9). Mise en place starts at 17:45, paced so the risotto's last ladle
lands as Ana's train does. At 19:05, presence: lights to 30% warm, the
dining scene, the *Quiet Dinner* playlist at 25% — merged from both your
tastes minus everything either of you skips on Fridays. Then the orb does
the most important thing: **it disappears.** Guest-quiet mode; the only
interruptions left are smoke, water, or the door. At 22:30 the kitchen
lights return for cleanup because the dishwasher door opened. One
collapsed receipt: *"Romantic dinner — 16 actions, $9, all reversible but
the saffron."*

**Ending B — out.** ⏳ *"Or I can get you into Foreign & Domestic at 8 —
they had a cancellation — and a car at 7:35."* Table booked (OpenTable),
Uber scheduled both ways, the flowers you didn't think of are waiting at
the table because it asked the restaurant, and the house goes to away-
secure behind you without anyone saying so. On the ride home it's already
warmed the living room by two degrees, because it's a cold night and it
knows how this evening ends: music, low light, and absolutely no cards on
any screen.

## The invisible machinery

### The continuous loop
Once a minute, forever: presence, safety, device health, printer state,
courier and trip tracking ⏳, routine schedules, deck readiness, budget
counters. Intentionally boring — 99% of ticks change nothing and say
nothing. The loop's product is *timing*: the right card at the right
moment, silence otherwise.

### The feedback loop
Deck thumbs reorder topics; approvals graduate to autonomy; corrections
become dated, superseding memories; skipped songs shape playlists; the
impulse item you always remove stops being added; undone receipts teach it
what it got wrong; every purchase's 👍/👎 tunes the next suggestion. None
of it is a black box — topic scores, earned permissions, budgets, and
memory are all inspectable surfaces.

### Memory — how it knows you
1. **Files** — MEMORY.md + topic files (household), `members/<you>.md`
   (personal). Readable, editable, deletable.
2. **Episodes** — a rolling log of the last days' real conversations.
3. **The dream** — every six hours it re-reads recent episodes and
   *extracts* the durable: preferences, people, projects, the brush-shed
   complaint that becomes March's gift idea. Facts are dated; conflicts
   supersede, never erase: *"espresso (until 2026-06); now flat white."*
4. **Auto-recall** — every message is semantically matched against
   everything it knows; the relevant memories ride into the turn silently.
   You never say "remember when." The most-used memories rank up.
   *"Forget everything about me"* is one tap and means it.

### The commerce layer ⏳ — the orb's hands
- **Connected services** link like Spotify: one tap, per member, tokens
  sealed on your box. Where a service has a real API, the orb drives it;
  where it doesn't, it builds the cart and hands you a filled checkout —
  one tap to pay, never a lie about what it did (appendix).
- **Budgets are policy**: per-category thresholds and caps, auto-tiers
  that must be *earned* like every autonomy, a weekly hard stop, and gifts
  permanently in always-ask. Members' spending obeys their profile —
  a child's orb doesn't order anything.
- **Replenishment engine**: consumption models per consumable (grams,
  cycles, cadence, "we're out" utterances) → threshold → cart → tier
  policy → receipt. Batching by store and by *trip*.
- **Every charge is a receipt** in the same ledger as the lights: amount,
  service, category, budget line it counted against, and the honest
  reversibility note — *refund path* instead of Undo where money moved.

### Self-improvement
Daily: the feedback loop — no code changes, a system that fits you better
each week. Structural: the orb edits its own source, validates in a
sandbox, promotes with rollback (owner-gated, receipted). Your
`.orbbackup` carries its whole self — memory, members, budgets, receipts,
even the Matter fabric — so the orb survives any rebuild with its
identity intact.

### Trust, restated
**Read** just happens. **Reversible** happens and carries its inverse.
**Consequential** asks first — and money is consequential until a
category has earned its tier. A hard core never automates: unlocking for
strangers, spending past caps, anything for a member whose profile says
no. The ledger is one tap away; Undo is real, and where it can't be
(money moved), the orb says so and knows the refund path instead.

## Appendix — service integrations & honest mechanisms ⏳

The experience above; the plumbing below. Three mechanism tiers, because
the orb never pretends:

- **API** — a real partner/consumer API exists; the orb drives it fully.
- **Handoff** — no purchase API; the orb builds the cart / deep-links the
  filled checkout, a human taps Pay. Still tracked, still receipted.
- **Watch** — the orb can track (email parsing, tracking numbers) even
  where it can't act.

| Service | Role in the playbook | Mechanism (2026 reality) | Trust tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uber / Uber Eats | rides, food | API (ride request); Eats: handoff | earned-auto / ask |
| Lyft | rides fallback, price check | handoff (public API retired) | earned-auto |
| DoorDash | food delivery | handoff (Drive API is B2B) | ask under cap |
| Instacart | groceries | API (developer platform) → else handoff | ask, review cart |
| Amazon | gifts, consumables, same-day | handoff (cart deep-link) + Watch (tracking) | consumables earned-auto; gifts ask |
| DigiKey / Mouser / McMaster | electronics & robot parts | API (DigiKey) / handoff, batched | earned-auto under cap |
| Filament stores | printer consumables | handoff + consumption model | earned-auto under cap |
| OpenTable / Resy | reservations | API/affiliate → else handoff | ask |
| Flowers (florist APIs) | day-of saves | handoff | always ask (gift) |
| Pharmacy (CVS et al.) | refills | Watch + handoff | ask |
| UPS / USPS / FedEx | deliveries, returns | Watch (tracking) + label printing via IPP | read |
| Subscriptions | the monthly audit | Watch (mail parsing) + cancel handoff | ask |

Build order follows the diary: **food-delivery handoff and ride booking
first** (highest daily frequency), then the replenishment engine
(filament/parts/groceries), then gifts & reservations, then the
watch-everything layer (deliveries, refunds, subscriptions) that makes
the ledger complete.
